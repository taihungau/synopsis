05Why now

Oil has an exchange. Compute has an email thread.

The identical chip, on the same day, quoted by fifteen different sellers. This is what a market looks like before anyone builds an exchange for it.

Why an exchange appears now and not before Every commodity got one when three things became true at once: the thing being sold became standard, lots of different people started selling it, and the price began moving faster than contracts could be renegotiated. All three are now true. The chip price fell 79%, then rose 38% in five months8 — and nobody could protect themselves against either move.

12What actually gets traded

One exchange, two credits, three sides.

A technical slide in the deck, and the idea underneath it is old and simple: you cannot run a market in promises from one particular supplier. So you standardise the promise, and trade the standard.

01 · The claim is what trades

A credit is a claim on one hour of a class of chip — not on a named supplier’s particular machine. This is exactly how metal exchanges work: you trade a warrant for a tonne of copper, not a specific pallet in a specific shed. Interchangeability is what creates a deep market.

02 · Two credits, one wallet

A buyer funds one balance in dollars and decides later whether to spend it on raw chip hours or on finished AI. Suppliers are paid in dollars. Because there is one settlement currency behind both, the whole thing is one accounting system rather than two.

03 · The rate between them

Finished AI is just chip hours already used. So the ratio between the two prices is a number every AI business is exposed to and nobody publishes. DeepSeek disclosed its own: $87,072 of chips a day produced $562,027 of output — 6.5×. Exascale would be where that rate is quoted.

The third side A marketplace has two sides: buyers and sellers. An exchange has a third — traders, who never want the chips at all and are there to profit from price movement. They are what makes a market deep enough that everyone else gets a fair price.

16Liquidity

Depth is the difference between a noticeboard and a market.

Liquidity means: can you buy or sell right now, in the size you want, without moving the price against yourself? It is the single property that separates a real exchange from a listings site — and the hardest thing to create.

Why this is the make-or-break slide Everything else in this deck can be bought with money and time. Liquidity cannot. An exchange with no traders has no price, and with no price there is no reason for the next trader to join. Every venue in history has faced this problem and most have failed it. Nothing has yet traded on this book.

17How the money is made

Three tolls on the same hour of computing.

Sell side · the chip cloud30% Of what we sell for a data centre

The operator keeps 70% and pays nothing for the software. They stop having to find their own customers. NVIDIA launched the identical structure in July 2026, committing 210,000 chips to two partners.

Buy side · the model service5–25× Markup on the raw cost

Finished AI sold at a multiple of the electricity and chip time behind it. Because Exascale is already metering the chips, that spread belongs to it rather than being a toll paid to someone else.

The venue · the exchange1.8% Of every trade that crosses

Charged to whoever takes a price off the screen; whoever posts one is paid a rebate. That asymmetry is deliberate — posting prices is what creates a market, so it is rewarded.

The whole model in one sentence Exascale charges for finished AI, charges for raw chip time, and charges a small percentage on the gap between them — and only the third one gets bigger as the shortage gets worse. Demand grows in multiples; supply grows in percentages; the spread between them is the product.

20Why it would be hard to copy

Each product feeds the next one.

The three products are not three businesses that happen to share an office. Each one creates the conditions the next one needs, which is why they are one company.

The honest caveat This is an argument, not a fact. It rests on the loop actually turning — and today it has not turned once, because nothing has traded. A moat that exists on a diagram is a hypothesis.

21Where it goes next

The fourth product is the price itself.

Once enough trading happens in one place, the price that place publishes becomes the number everyone else writes into their contracts — whether or not they trade there. That is what Brent means for oil.
