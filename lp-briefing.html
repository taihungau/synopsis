02Purpose

Compute is becoming the world’s next major commodity.

AI consumes compute the way industry consumes energy. But compute is not traded as a commodity is traded: it is fragmented across providers, priced in private contracts, hard to compare, impossible to hedge and difficult to lend against.

The entire company in four words
OnePrice

A continuous, public reference price for a standardised unit of compute.

TwoTrade

Spot and forward markets where that price is discovered, not negotiated.

ThreeHedge

Derivatives that transfer compute price risk between buyer and operator.

FourFinance

Credit written against capacity, underwritten on an observable curve.

The thesis in one line Every major commodity followed the same progression — physical asset, standardised price, spot market, derivatives, financing, global financial market. Compute is early in that sequence and moving through it unusually fast. Exascale exists to build the layer it is moving into.

The precise gap Compute already has everything a commodity market needs except the market itself: real supply, real demand, real scarcity, real price volatility. What it lacks is a venue, a benchmark and a curve. Oil had all three by 1988; compute has none of them in 2026.

The honest position Exascale does not have this category to itself and should not claim to. CME has validated the asset class and will own the first listed contract. What is unbuilt is everything around it: physical spot delivery, continuous exposure, capacity financing, and the settlement layer connecting a financial contract to a running machine. CME is a futures exchange; it does not deliver compute. That is the opening.

08Why Exascale wins

The exchange becomes the reference point for compute.

Why the loop is the defensible thing Each stage is the precondition for the next, and the last returns to the first. Liquidity produces a trustworthy benchmark; a benchmark makes derivatives writable; derivatives make capacity financeable; financing and trading generate the data that makes the benchmark better still. A competitor can copy any single stage. None works alone, and the venue that assembles the whole loop becomes the price everyone quotes against — the position ICE holds in oil and CME holds in rates.

The distinction that decides this slide Spot and derivatives are different businesses with take rates three orders of magnitude apart. A physical marketplace earns 100–180 bp because it delivers a good; a futures exchange earns a fraction of a basis point because it transfers risk at enormous scale. $30T of notional at listed-futures economics is revenue in the tens of millions, not $300B. Any model multiplying derivatives volume by a spot rate is wrong. Treat every figure here as illustrative economics at scale, never as forecast.
